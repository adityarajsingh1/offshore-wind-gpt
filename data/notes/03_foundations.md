# Foundations: fixed and floating

The type of foundation depends mostly on water depth and seabed conditions.

Monopiles are the most common foundation for offshore wind. A monopile is a single large steel tube driven or drilled into the seabed, with a transition piece on top that connects to the tower. Modern monopiles can be more than 10 metres in diameter and weigh well over 1,000 tonnes. They are usually used in water depths up to around 50 to 60 metres.

Jacket foundations are steel lattice structures with three or four legs, pinned to the seabed with piles or suction buckets. They are stiffer and lighter for their size than monopiles and are often used in deeper water, roughly 40 to 60 metres, and for offshore substations.

Gravity-based foundations are heavy concrete structures that sit on the seabed and are held in place by their own weight. Suction buckets (suction caissons) are large upside-down steel buckets that are pushed into soft seabed by pumping water out of them, which avoids loud pile driving.

Pile driving is noisy, and the underwater noise can harm marine mammals such as harbour porpoises. Projects often use noise mitigation such as bubble curtains, and some countries set strict noise limits.

Scour is the erosion of seabed sediment around a foundation caused by currents and waves. It is controlled with scour protection, usually layers of rock placed around the base.

Floating offshore wind is used where the water is too deep for fixed foundations, generally beyond about 60 metres. The turbine sits on a floating platform held in place by mooring lines and anchors. The main platform types are the spar buoy (a long, deep cylinder kept upright by ballast), the semi-submersible (several connected columns that float partly submerged) and the tension-leg platform (held down by taut vertical tendons). Hywind Scotland, which started operating in 2017, was the world's first commercial floating wind farm, with five 6 MW turbines on spar buoys. Floating wind opens up deep-water areas such as off Norway, Japan, the US West Coast and the Mediterranean.
